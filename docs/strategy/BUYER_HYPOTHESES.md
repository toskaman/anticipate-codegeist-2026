This is a research plan, not customer evidence. Competitive research is owned by ORP-31 (#136) in `COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md`; do not duplicate or substitute its verified-source analysis. No interviews, comparative human task timings or willingness-to-pay results have been collected.

## Buyer hypotheses to test

| Persona | Specific problem / buying objection | Current proof to show | Missing purchase proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CTO | Repeated consequential changes with no reconstructable decision; another tool may add process | One real change, causal sources, stale approval refusal, Passport | Adoption burden, organization-wide visibility, real pilot outcomes |
| VP Engineering | Ownership across teams; shared approvals may be assumed available | Current private workflow plus honest shared-workspace limitation | Hosted separation of duties and two-account proof |
| Engineering manager | Reviewers see tickets, not indirect consumers; setup cost | Guided demo, named consequences and readable reason chain | Human completion/error observations on their data |
| Platform lead | Retire/upgrade a shared service without losing downstream context | Direct/transitive Jira paths and migration assumptions | Data coverage beyond existing Jira links; missing links remain unknown |
| Enterprise architect | Need provenance and reproducibility, not a risk percentage | Snapshot, source references and deterministic result hash | Integration breadth, retention and procurement review |
| Release manager | Understand a delay and distinguish approved from actually migrated | Dated sample slip, action verification and separate gate state | Hosted clean-project sample result and an authentic release dataset |
| Risk/governance lead | Reconstruct who approved which facts; concern about authority/integrity | Recorded approval scope, refusal on changed evidence, historical labelling | Approved audit policy; Passport is not a cryptographic certificate |

### Positioning

ANTICIPATE helps engineering teams review consequential Jira changes with traceable evidence, deterministic alternatives, explicit approval and verified outcomes. The near-term wedge is a platform team retiring or upgrading a shared API whose consumers span linked issues. This does not replace Jira Plans, JSM change management or missing operational data.

### Packaging and pricing hypotheses, not an offer

- **Evaluation:** the current private single-change workflow and explicitly approved sample dataset. Test installation friction, comprehension and a buyer's own change before proposing a price.
- **Team workflow (future):** shared reviews and separation of duties only after #131 is shipped and hosted-tested. Investigate a site-based subscription versus active-reviewer licensing; ask how it fits Atlassian procurement and site user counts.
- **Governance (future):** monitored scope, export policy and portfolio review only after #130/#132 and an agreed retention/authorization model. Test whether these change a buying decision; do not price imaginary features.

No public list price, checkout, savings estimate, customer count or demand claim is set. For each pilot record the buyer role, alternative used today, security/procurement blocker, actual task evidence and willingness to pay. A stated preference is not a purchase. A paid pilot is not a recurring revenue forecast.

## Pitch variants grounded in current functionality

**Product:** See what depends on a Jira change, compare the consequences and keep a record of what was approved and verified.

**Developer:** A Forge-native Jira workflow built around source-linked evidence, deterministic simulations, explicit mutation approval, freshness checks and readback verification. Unknown evidence stays unknown.

**Executive:** Before a consequential change proceeds, make its assumptions and dependencies reviewable. Afterwards, retain the decision, approval scope and verified outcomes. Coverage and permissions remain visible; no claim promises incident prevention in every case.
